# GRC Reflections

## Reflection 01 — From Specialist to System Thinker

Early phases focus on the individual part: requirements, controls, roles and evidence.

Later phases reveal that the difficult problems often occur **between** functions.

**Reflection:** GRC value increases when the practitioner can see the connections between specialist activities.

## Reflection 02 — The Handoff Is a Risk Point

A process can fail even when both departments perform their own tasks correctly if the transition between them is weak.

**Reflection:** investigate the interface, not only the individual function.

## Reflection 03 — Evidence Changes How You Design Processes

Once evidence and auditability are considered during implementation, process design becomes more deliberate.

**Reflection:** ask “How will we prove this?” before the audit arrives.

## Reflection 04 — Coordination Has Decision Boundaries

The conductor may see the problem without having authority to make the final decision.

**Reflection:** good coordination includes knowing when to escalate rather than silently taking ownership.

## Reflection 05 — Resilience Exposes Hidden Dependencies

The Injury phase showed that a process that works under normal conditions can depend heavily on one person.

**Reflection:** disruption is a useful way to reveal hidden single points of failure.

## Reflection 06 — Audit Is a Feedback Mechanism

Audit is not merely a final inspection. It produces information about whether the system is actually operating as intended.

**Reflection:** assurance should feed improvement.

## Final Mental Model

```mermaid
flowchart LR
 A[Business Objective] --> B[Requirements]
 B --> C[Risk / Control Need]
 C --> D[Control]
 D --> E[Owner]
 E --> F[Implementation]
 F --> G[Evidence]
 G --> H[Testing]
 H --> I[Audit]
 I --> J[Finding]
 J --> K[Corrective Action]
 K --> L[Improvement]
 L --> A
```

> **The conductor's job is not to make every sound personally. It is to keep the whole system moving toward the intended outcome.**
