# Stakeholders

## OrchestraX Stakeholder Model

The project is deliberately small enough to simulate, but broad enough to demonstrate cross-functional coordination.

| Stakeholder | Primary Interest | Typical Responsibility | Conductor Interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive Sponsor | Business outcome, risk, resources | Direction and decisions | Reporting, escalation, decisions |
| GRC / Project Lead | Governance, coordination, assurance | Plan, coordinate, track, report | Central coordination point |
| IT Lead | Technology operations | Technical implementation | Dependencies, evidence, technical decisions |
| Security Lead | Security controls | Security implementation and validation | Risk, control design, testing |
| HR Lead | People processes | Joiner/mover/leaver processes | People-data dependencies |
| Operations Lead | Business operations | Operational process execution | Process dependencies |
| Legal / Privacy | Regulatory and privacy requirements | Legal interpretation and privacy controls | Requirements and consultation |
| Internal Audit | Independent assurance | Testing and findings | Evidence and audit coordination |
| Control Owners | Individual controls | Control operation and evidence | Ownership, follow-up, escalation |

## Stakeholder Questions

For each stakeholder, the conductor should know:

- What outcome do they own?
- What information do they need?
- What information do they provide?
- What decisions can they make?
- What dependencies do they create?
- What happens if their input is late?

## Key Principle

A stakeholder is not automatically a control owner.

Someone may influence a requirement, execute part of a process, approve a decision, or provide assurance without owning the underlying control.

## Learning Exercise

Select one ISO-aligned control and identify:

- Sponsor
- Accountable owner
- Responsible executor
- Consulted specialists
- Informed stakeholders
- Evidence provider
- Assurance function
